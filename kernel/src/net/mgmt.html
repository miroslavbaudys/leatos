Interfaces

Physical ports and the router's addresses on them.

Ports

Egress queues

Each port's transmit side has its own queue (CAKE by default); settings under Traffic shaping.

Networks on the LAN port

Router interfaces on lan0, untagged or on a VLAN: configured under Networks.

Clients

Devices with a DHCP lease from the router.

Networks

Each network is a subnet on the LAN port, untagged or on its own VLAN, with its own DHCP pool and a firewall zone (for example a guest or IoT network).

Networks

The first network is the main LAN: edit it under LAN & DHCP.

Adding, removing or changing a network (VLAN, address, zone) applies at once, without a reboot; changing the address of the network you use for this page boots as a trial. A VLAN needs a switch or access point that tags it on the LAN cable.

WAN

The internet uplink.

Status

Only if the ISP requires a tagged link. A change restarts the WAN.

Connection

A change restarts the WAN (DHCP client or PPPoE session). The PPPoE password is never shown.

LAN & DHCP

The local network, its DHCP server and fixed addresses.

Network

DNS

The router forwards LAN queries to the WAN's DNS servers and answers local names itself.

Status

Forwarder

Firewall

Stateful: replies to tracked connections always pass; new connections follow the policy below.

Zones in effect

Rows: where a new connection starts; columns: where it goes. Networks of one zone always reach each other. New TCP connections must start with a SYN; protocols other than TCP, UDP and ICMP are dropped for new flows. The WAN zone is fixed: nothing new comes in from the internet except through port forwards (below).

Zones

Built in: wan, and lan (everything to the router, forwards to the WAN) until you define a zone named lan.

"To the router: drop" refuses everything from that zone except the services ticked (DHCP is needed for clients to get an address). Changes apply to new connections at once.

Rules

Checked from the top for each new connection (and for traffic to the router); the first match decides, otherwise the zones do. Applied at once, to new connections.

"To" router means traffic for the router itself (its web interface, DNS, …); any matches forwarded traffic and the router. Replies to the router's own requests (DHCP, DNS upstream, time) always pass. reject drops for now. Rules to the router boot as a trial.

Port forwards

New connections to the router's WAN address on these ports go to a device inside; no extra rule needed. Applied at once.

Target: address (same ports) or address:port (a range maps onto port, port+1, …). Hairpin: devices inside reach it through the WAN address too. Ports 60000–65535 are the router's own.

Dropped packets by reason

Counted since boot by the forwarding plane (packets the router forwards, not its own).

Traffic shaping

Set the rates a little below the line's real speed, so the queue builds in the router (where CAKE keeps latency low) instead of in the modem.

Rates

The WAN's VLAN tag (+4 B) and PPPoE header (+8 B) are added to the overhead automatically.

Queue

Another queue discipline, fairness or ECN setting rebuilds the queue (packets in it are dropped once).
